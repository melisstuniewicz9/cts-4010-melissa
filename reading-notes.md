#### What is it?

Project Bamboo was a failed attempt at a cyberinfastructure initiative formed by the Andrew W. Mellon foundation (2008-2012). Aimed to enhance arts and humanities research through the development of infastructure and support for shared technology services.
* Brought together scholars, librarians and IT staff from several institutions
* Did not connect with scholars.
  * Perhaps the scholars did not have the technical literacy required to advocate for such an infastructure, or maybe it just did not appear to be of use to them
  * Buidling the infastructure is the easiest part about creating digital projects, what's difficult is cultivating a community who would advocate for it and susatain it through continuous use.
  * Another thing, from my understaning DH did not gain widespread academic traction till the mid 2000s. Maybe Bamboo was ahead of its time? Before finishing the article, I thought to myself, LINCS and CWRC seem to be a new and improved edition of Bamboo. Did those who work in infastructure consult Bamboo wikis?
  * Good that Bamboo, depsite its lack of success, kept records on its methodology, allows others to reproduce
* Funding constraints eliminated community aspect of project

**Overarching theme:** Working together in teams from different professional communities is difficult, must share same or similar visions in order to experience success. 

**What UChicago's IT team was trying to do (value proposition:)**
* Spend less time on delveloping technology and more time on scholarship
* Too many wesbites, too many databases
* Reinventing technologies that existed prior (reinvent the wheel)

Bamboo proposal (UC Berkeley and UChicago) seeked participation from librarians, humanities researchers, computer science researchers
* Recognized the diversity in these communities, but I guess they tried putting them into a box bases on common ground?
* Workshops were held, but did not go smoothly, required technical literacy most simply did not have
 * Workshop 1 participants could not understand Wiki
 * Felt that administrators failed to account for the fact that research is dynamic, not a set of practices
 * Too many goals, did not stick to a tight and practical problem

## Blunt Instrumentalism - On Tools and Methods

<img width="992" height="594" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/2d8d43a3-1ce8-4281-a606-9aa3265b883f" />

Humanities can be a place not just to think about things, but to do things (making)
* "Doing" expands scope of critical activity, provides students with practical work experience, and can help enact meaningful change
* **Challenges:** Not finding an audience, lack of impact

Stop prioritizing big software in academia, we are better off tinkering and experimenting

**Overarching idea:** Need to make tools that enourage critical engagement with methodology
* Broken telescope example: If we don't understand what makes it work, how do we know if our results have merit or are reproducable?
* We should understand tools better than the tool makers, or at the very least, understand their strengths, klimitations and biases
*  Best tools are ones we make ourselves

**NLTK:**
* K-clustering, don't trust algorithms blindly

**Unplanned Obsolescence:**
* Easy to create software, but academic attention spans are cyclical, interest wavers
* Dependencies break and plaatforms decay
* No amount of engineering and beautiful web design can guarantee participation and community
* Hard to find time to create advocacy: costs time and money that faculty just doesn't have
 * Training, fundraising, focus groups and interviews (to see what works and what doesn't, suggestions for improvement)
 * Built tool, but not community around it

**"Tools are great when they save time, but not when they shield us from the complexity of thought"**
